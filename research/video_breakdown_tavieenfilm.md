### Analysis
- **Hook (0:00–0:15):** a personal, second-person grievance ("You waited 16 years… and your dad tracks it"), backed by one huge number on screen. Interpretation: it lands on a universal teen memory within 3 s.
- **Central question:** which parenting style actually produces honesty? The title promises a counter-intuitive answer ("still didn't know").
- **Structure:** a *countdown* told in clock times. Each chapter is a timestamp, so the viewer always knows "how close" the climax is. Tension peaks at 11:45–11:46 (the door opens; "the next 10 seconds").
- **Curiosity:** withholds what the dad does in those 10 seconds; then a **rewind** ("Go back to 11:46. Same kid, same party") into a split-screen comparison (Dad 1 vs Dad 2).
- **Fact layer:** study cards with highlighted lines and stats sit *inside* the story, not in a lecture section.
- **Payoff and CTA:** "Which dad did you get?" makes the comment section a self-report.
- **Script:** short declarative sentences, second person, present tense, minimal dialogue ("Sam's? You say one word"). Captions are always on.
- **Visuals:** one faceless-minimal white character (cheap to keep consistent), mood lighting by scene (purple night, warm lamp), huge typographic numbers, document and study cards, split screens. Interpretation: deliberately low-cost rigs + lighting + typography = high perceived production value.
- **Editing and sound:** captions mark "[music]" throughout, so there's a continuous score (not heard). Shot length isn't measurable from stills: **unavailable**.
- **Packaging:** "Why [relatable group] Still [didn't get the expected result]" plus a single emoji (📍 = location) that encodes the topic.

## Reusable mechanics (extracted, not copied)
1. Second-person present tense ("You…").
2. A frame device that gives a sense of progress (clock countdown / numbered rooms / suspects).
3. One giant on-screen number or phrase per beat.
4. Rewind / split-screen "same kid, different choice".
5. Fiction disclaimer plus real sources, which builds trust.
6. A CTA question that makes viewers self-identify in comments.
