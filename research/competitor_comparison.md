# tavieenfilm vs Money Decoded: comparison

(O) = observed · (I) = interpretation or hypothesis

| Dimension | tavieenfilm | Money Decoded |
|---|---|---|
| Subject | Family psychology, everyday teen/parent conflict (O) | Corporate finance and power (O) |
| Point of view | **Second person, "You"** (O) | Third person, named real executives (O) |
| Fact vs fiction | Explicit: "The story is fiction. The findings come from real studies" + citations (O) | Real people in a dramatised storyline, sources not shown in what I read (O) |
| Frame device | Clock-time countdown / rooms + score / investigation (O) | News chapters + an A/B board decision (O) |
| Visual system | Minimal white 3D figure, cinematic mood lighting, giant numbers, study cards, split screens (O) | Flat vector chibi caricatures, chalkboard palette, icon cards, title cards (O) |
| Emotional engine | Personal grievance, fear of the parent's reaction, identity ("Which dad did you get?") (I) | Corporate drama, "your wallet" stake (I) |
| Public traction | 3.1M / 934K / 77K (O) | 2.4K (O) |

## What each does better
- **tavieenfilm:** universal relatability; the countdown structure; trust through disclaimers and sources; cinematic lighting on cheap rigs; comment-driving identity questions.
- **Money Decoded:** clean chaptering with title cards; an A/B decision ending; turning an abstract metric into a mystery ("the fourteenth week"); a very fast template for producing.

## Shared techniques
A personal-stakes hook in the first sentence · numbers as visual punctuation · always-on burned-in captions · chapters · a closing question or vote.

## Transferable to MOTHWAKE
1. **Clock-time countdown chapters** (11:30 Someone knocks · 11:45 Your door opens). It's native to horror.
2. **Second-person present tense** ("You hear him on the stairs") for immersion.
3. **Rewind / split-screen alternate outcome**, which fits our two-part A/B format.
4. **Fiction disclaimer + real science** as a trust layer ("The story is fiction. The fear response is real.").
5. **Identity CTA** ("Which door would you have opened?").
6. **Giant single-word or number cards** in brand type.

## Do NOT copy
- Their topics, scripts, characters, the white-figure look, or their title wording.
- Real-people dramatisation presented as news (Money Decoded's risk).
- The family-psychology niche itself. MOTHWAKE stays horror.

## Efficiency (I)
Money Decoded's flat template is the cheapest to produce but visually thin. tavieenfilm's minimal 3D figure plus lighting is the better quality-per-cost model, and the closest analogue is our **one recurring cast + reusable rooms + Flow clips + type cards**.

## New transferable techniques from Paper Billions (mechanics only)
- **Cold open on the most dramatic moment**, then "to understand why, you need to know who…".
- **Physical metaphors** for abstract ideas (org chart as a building). In horror: e.g. a building's floor plan drawn as the "rules" map.
- **Evidence board / paper-headline collage** as a recurring "Investigation" visual, drawn in MOTHWAKE colours and fonts (our own design).
- **A recurring anchor character** (their night analyst). Ours: Ivy, already established.

## Explicitly not adopted
Real-people dramatisation · fully automated "pick a topic and it builds the video" pipelines (policy risk, no originality) · gambling/high-leverage sponsors · copying titles or topic feeds.
