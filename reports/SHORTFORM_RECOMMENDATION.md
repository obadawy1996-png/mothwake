# Is short-form micro horror stronger than the cinematic direction?

**Date:** 2026-11-19 · **Evidence:** `research/shortform/` (47 channels, 476 Shorts, 320 Google Trends rows, 328 YouTube autocomplete rows; measured 2026-11-19) and the earlier long-form research.

## Short answer

**For the goal you set (higher engagement, fast), yes: short-form is stronger.** On its own it is weaker for **revenue and loyal subscribers**. The strongest plan is a **hybrid**: Shorts are the engine for discovery and testing, and a few long-form stories expand whatever the Shorts prove people want.

## Recommendation

1. **Lead with short-form.** Run the 3-video pilot, then scale toward 30 if the pilot passes. Use the data-backed defaults:
   - sleep/waking and winter originals
   - original-world rules involving family or everyday objects
   - two-part "comment A or B" dilemmas
   - question-mark, family and door titles
   - no 3AM/DON'T titles
2. **Hit the seasonal window.** Winter and Christmas Shorts live by about **14 December** (under-supplied, peaks around 21 December).
3. **Keep long-form, but earn it.** Once a Short topic or character clearly outperforms, expand it into a long-form story roughly every 3–4 weeks. That is the route to watch hours, revenue and loyal subscribers. *The Quiet Hour* stays on hold as the template.
4. **Protect the channel.** Original stories, a steady and sustainable volume, a visible human-made craft, and AI disclosure where required.

## Limits

- **No Meta, TikTok or Instagram data.** Every one of those sites was blocked from here. Real numbers will come from your own account insights once the accounts exist.
- **YouTube's bot check** limited the detailed per-Short data (duration known for 34 of 43 channels).
- **Revenue-per-view figures** come from secondary search summaries.
- **No promise.** Nothing here guarantees views. The data only tells us where to place our bets first.
