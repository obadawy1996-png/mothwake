# EP001 — The Quiet Hour (script v3)

**Story:** MW012 · **Format:** long-form 16:9 · **Target:** 9:30–11:00 · **Narrator:** Cal (first person, podcast voice)
**Status:** draft for owner review · original fiction written for MOTHWAKE

Conventions: **NARR** = Cal's voiceover · **CAL** = Cal speaking in scene · **ECHO** = the thing speaking in borrowed voices (processed in post: flattened, slightly late, as if heard through a wall) · **[ ]** = picture and sound · `(beat)` ≈ 0.8 s of silence.

---

## COLD OPEN (0:00–0:30)

[Black. A diner clock ticks. 12:59:58. Warm Lamp light swings into frame: a booth, coffee steam, a waitress mid-pour, two truckers mid-laugh.]

[12:59:59. The second hand lands on 1:00. **Every sound in the diner stops at once.** The laugh cuts off. Nobody moves their mouth. Only the coffee keeps running, over the rim of the cup and onto the counter.]

**NARR:** In Wrenfield, nobody speaks between one and two in the morning. (beat)

**NARR:** Not to answer a phone. Not to call for help. (beat) Not even in their sleep.

[Outside the window, the radio tower on the hill blinks its red light once, slowly.]

**NARR:** I went there to find out why. (beat) I should have just listened.

## TITLE (0:30–0:36)

[Title card: A MOTHWAKE STORY · *The Quiet Hour*. Signature sound.]

## ACT I — THE RULE (0:36–3:50)

[Highway at night. A hatchback with a dented door. Rain on the windscreen.]

**NARR:** I make a podcast about small towns with strange habits. Most of them turn out to be nothing. A festival nobody remembers starting. A grudge between two families. A founder with a funny will. (beat) I'd done forty-one episodes. People liked them because nothing bad ever happened at the end.

[A town-line sign in the headlights: WRENFIELD. POP. 412. Beneath it, hand-painted on a board: VISITORS — PLEASE LEARN TO BE QUIET.]

**NARR:** Wrenfield's habit was already on the sign. That's rare. Usually you have to dig.

[A motel office. A bell on the counter with a strip of tape over the clapper. The owner, an older man, slides a key across and taps a laminated card: QUIET HOUR 1–2 A.M. THANK YOU FOR YOUR COOPERATION.]

**NARR:** The motel owner didn't explain it. He just tapped the card twice and watched me until I nodded. (beat) It was nine in the evening. He could have talked. He chose not to.

[A gas station under one flickering tube light. An attendant in his twenties. A hand-lettered sign above the till: WE CLOSE OUR MOUTHS AT ONE. THANK YOU.]

**NARR:** The kid at the gas station was more relaxed about it. He said it was like Sunday church, or not walking on the cracks. Something your grandparents did, so you did it too. (beat) Then I asked what happened to people who didn't.

[The attendant's smile stays exactly where it is. He counts out Cal's change very slowly.]

**NARR:** He counted my change twice. And he never answered the question. (beat) He just said, "You're not staying long, are you?" And it didn't sound like a question either.

[Main street. Everything closed except one diner, its windows the only warm light in town.]

**NARR:** The only place open after midnight was a diner called the Lantern. The woman who ran it was called Ines. (beat) She didn't speak at all. Not just during the hour. Ever.

[Ines behind the counter. Fifties, steady eyes, a notepad on a string around her neck. She writes and turns it to camera: *COFFEE?*]

**NARR:** She wrote everything on a little pad she wore around her neck. When I asked her why the town went silent, she wrote one word.

[Notepad: *HABIT.*]

**NARR:** When I asked what happens if you talk, she tore the page off, folded it in half, and put it in her pocket. (beat) Like it was something that shouldn't be left lying around.

[Cal's recorder on the counter, red light on. He checks the levels: a green bar jumping with the jukebox.]

**NARR:** I stayed until one. I wanted to hear it for myself.

[12:57. Customers finish sentences early. A man stops a story halfway and just nods. A trucker pays in exact change so he won't have to ask for any.]

**NARR:** You could feel the whole room getting ready. People said goodbye to each other at two minutes to one, even though nobody was leaving.

[1:00. Silence. The fridge hum. The clock. Cal's recorder level drops to almost nothing.]

**NARR:** It's hard to describe how loud a room full of people being quiet is. (beat) It isn't calm. It's effort. Everybody holding something in.

[Ines looks at Cal. She writes slowly and turns the pad: *NOT A WORD. PLEASE.*]

[Through the window, the tower light blinks. Then again. Steady, like breathing.]

**NARR:** At two o'clock, everyone exhaled at once, and the room went back to normal, as if a song had ended. (beat) I didn't say a word that first night. I want that on the record.

---

## ACT II — THE ECHO (3:50–7:30)

[Cal's motel room. Cheap lamp. Laptop. Headphones on. He plays back the night.]

**NARR:** Back at the motel I went through the recording. Twelve fifty-nine: chatter, cups, the jukebox. One o'clock: nothing. Just the fridge and forty people breathing.

[Waveform on screen: flat. Then, at 1:37, a tiny blip.]

**NARR:** Except at one thirty-seven. (beat) Something very small.

[He boosts the gain. The blip is a breath. Then a sound like a mouth trying a shape it doesn't know.]

**ECHO:** (barely, wrong) …ffee…

**NARR:** It sounded like someone practising the word *coffee*. (beat) Nobody in that diner had moved their lips. I'd been watching.

[Day. Main street, bright and friendly. People talk, laugh, wave. A kid rings a bike bell.]

**NARR:** In daylight, Wrenfield was the friendliest town I've ever been to. Everybody talked. Too much, almost. As if they were getting it all out of their system before night came.

[The town library. A drawer of cassette tapes labelled by year: 1974, 1981, 1996, 2009.]

**NARR:** The library kept tapes. Interviews with visitors, recorded by a town historian who died in 2011. (beat) Every tape was a stranger who'd come to ask about the quiet hour.

[A listening booth. Tape 1974. A man laughing, nervous.]

**VISITOR 1974 (on tape):** So what happens if I talk? Hello? *Hello?*

**NARR:** Each tape ended the same way. The visitor talked during the hour. On purpose, as a joke, or by accident. And afterwards, the historian went outside and recorded the silence.

[Tape 1981, post-hour: wind, then faintly:]

**ECHO:** (rough, a stranger's voice) …hello…

[Tape 1996:]

**ECHO:** (another voice) …what happens if I…

[Tape 2009, clearer, nearly a sentence:]

**ECHO:** (another voice) …is anybody… out there…

**NARR:** It wasn't just repeating them. (beat) It was keeping them. Every visitor who spoke left it a few more words. Fifty years of tourists, teaching it one sentence at a time.

[Afternoon. A porch. An old woman shelling peas. Cal holds out his recorder. She looks at it the way you'd look at a dog that might bite.]

**NARR:** I tried to get someone to say it on the record. An old woman on Fenner Street, who'd lived in Wrenfield for eighty years, agreed to talk to me, as long as I turned off the recorder first.

[Cal clicks it off and shows her. She leans in close.]

**NARR:** She told me that when she was a girl, the town still had a night-time radio show. A local station, broadcasting through the small hours. Requests, weather, a man reading the news. (beat) One winter, the station kept broadcasting after the man went home.

[A flashback in faded colour: a tiny radio booth, an empty chair, the microphone light still on. Through a frosted window, the tower light blinks.]

**NARR:** Nobody was in the booth. But for one hour every night, something kept talking. Bits of the request show. Bits of the weather. Bits of people's names. (beat) They tore the transmitter out in the spring. The tower's still there. The town has been quiet ever since.

[Back on the porch. She picks up a pea pod and splits it with her thumbnail.]

**NARR:** I asked her whether it was still out there. She said, "Honey, it's always been out there. We just stopped giving it anything to say."

[Cal in his car, recorder in his lap, lit by the dashboard.]

**NARR:** I should have left then. (beat) But I'm a podcaster. Talking is the only thing I've ever been any good at. And I had the best story of my life sitting in a drawer in a library.

---

### The second night

[The diner. 12:58. Ines sees Cal's recorder. She shakes her head, a small, frightened no.]

**NARR:** The second night, I talked.

[1:00. Silence falls. Cal leans close to his recorder.]

**CAL:** It's one o'clock in Wrenfield, and I'm going to say something.

[Every head in the diner turns. Nobody speaks. A woman covers her child's ears.]

[Outside, beyond the glass, a faint fog of breath appears, as if someone is standing very close to the window.]

**ECHO:** (Cal's voice, flat and late, from outside) …it's one o'clock… in…

[Cal freezes. The fog on the glass slowly fades.]

**NARR:** It used my voice. (beat) Not well. Like someone wearing a coat that doesn't fit yet.

[Ines writes, hands shaking, and doesn't turn the pad. She just closes her eyes.]

[2:00. The room exhales. Nobody looks at Cal. People leave money on the tables and go home without finishing their food.]

**NARR:** At two o'clock, nobody said goodbye. They just left. (beat) Ines locked the door behind the last of them. Then she pointed at me, at the door, and at the road out of town. It was the clearest sentence anyone said to me in Wrenfield.

---

## ACT III — FLUENCY (7:30–10:00)

[Motel. Night three. Cal hasn't slept. Notes all over the bed: dates, tapes, a hand-drawn timeline.]

**NARR:** The third night I didn't go to the diner. I stayed in my room with every light on, and I said nothing at all.

[1:12 on the bedside clock. Through the motel wall, faint and conversational:]

**ECHO:** (Cal's voice, smoother) …I make a podcast about small towns… with strange habits…

**NARR:** That was my intro. Word for word. (beat) It had been listening the whole time. Not just during the hour. In the car. In the motel. Every time I talked to myself.

[1:24. Cal's phone buzzes on the bed. Caller ID: HOME. He stares at it. It rings out.]

[A voicemail notification. He doesn't play it. The phone plays it on speaker by itself.]

**ECHO:** (Cal's voice, almost natural) Hey, it's me. (beat) Pick up. I want to tell you about the town.

**NARR:** I live alone. (beat) The only person who calls me from home is me, when I've left my phone somewhere and I'm trying to find it.

[Cal opens his laptop. His podcast dashboard. A draft episode he never made: "WRENFIELD — Part 1". Length: 41 minutes.]

**NARR:** And it had found the one place where a voice can go on talking without anybody in the room.

[Morning. The Lantern. Ines writes fast, for the first time. Page after page, torn off and handed to Cal.]

[Notepad pages, one by one:]
*IT CAN'T FINISH IF WE DON'T TEACH IT.*
*EVERY VISITOR GAVE IT WORDS.*
*YOU GAVE IT MORE THAN ANYONE.*
*IT ONLY NEEDS THE LAST ONES.*

**NARR:** I asked her what happens when it finishes. (beat) She didn't write anything. She just looked at the radio behind the counter, the way you look at a door you're not allowed to open.

**NARR:** The silence was never a superstition. (beat) It was a wall. And one word at a time, people like me had been taking it down.

[Cal at his car. He puts the recorder in the glovebox and locks it. Deletes the draft episode. The dashboard asks: *Are you sure?* He clicks yes.]

**NARR:** So I decided to leave without saying another word. Not to Ines. Not to the man at the gas station. Not even to myself. (beat) I deleted the episode. I'd make something else. Something where nothing bad happens at the end.

[The town line at 12:55 a.m. The sign: VISITORS — PLEASE LEARN TO BE QUIET. The car's tail lights shrink into the dark.]

[A long, held silence. Four seconds. The tower light blinks.]

---

## ENDING (10:00–10:50)

[Ines's flat above the diner. 1:30 a.m. She sits in the dark beside a small radio.]

[The radio crackles. A podcast intro. A warm, confident, intimate voice. It is Cal's voice. Fluent. Perfect.]

**ECHO:** (as Cal, flawless, warm) I make a podcast about small towns with strange habits. (beat) This week: a town called Wrenfield. I'm here now. (beat) And I'm going to talk for as long as you'll let me.

[Ines reaches for the dial. Her hand stops. The voice is coming from the radio, but also, very faintly, from the hallway outside her door.]

[Hold on the door. A moth lands on the window behind her, lit by the radio dial.]

[Cut to black.]

[Outro sting. End screen: two related stories.]

## Notes for review

- **Narration length** is measured by `scripts/production/script_stats.py`. With the written silences and visual-only beats, the target runtime is about 9:30–10:50. Final timing comes from the recorded narration.
- **Twist set-up and payoff:** the tower light in the cold open, the 1:37 blip, the tapes, the intro heard through the wall, the draft episode, then the ending. The ending recontextualises Cal's opening line, "I make a podcast…", which the Echo repeats fluently.
- **Originality note:** "a thing that mimics voices" is a broad folklore trope. Language spreading through a radio station is the premise of an existing film, *Pontypool* (2008). To stay distinct, MOTHWAKE has **no infection**, the radio station appears **only as a short backstory flashback** (the main settings are a diner, a motel and a library), and no character is a DJ. The Echo is a separate entity that learns from outsiders, the town's rule is a defence, and the horror is that the narrator's own podcast becomes its mouth. Keep this distinction in any revision.
- **Brand:** low gore, sound as the horror device, the moth easter egg in the final frame, the Lamp-lit diner against the dark town.
- **No real-world claims:** Wrenfield is fictional.
